Types of Errors

  • Medication errors — most common; wrong drug/dose/route/patient
  • Surgical errors — wrong site, procedure, or patient
  • Diagnostic errors — misdiagnosis, delayed diagnosis
  • Communication errors — during handoffs, between providers
  • Infection control failures — HAIs, improper technique

Prevention Strategies

  • CPOE — reduces handwriting errors
  • Barcode scanning — right patient, right med
  • Surgical time-out — before every procedure
  • Medication reconciliation — at care transitions
  • Teach-back — confirm patient understanding
  • SBAR — structured handoffs

PQRST Waves

  • P wave — atrial depolarization
  • PR interval — AV conduction (normal 0.12–0.20 sec)
  • QRS complex — ventricular depolarization (normal <0.12 sec)
  • ST segment — ventricular repolarization begins (elevation = MI)
  • T wave — ventricular repolarization
  • QT interval — total ventricular activity (prolonged = Torsades risk)

Primary vs. Secondary HTN

  • Primary (essential): 90–95%; no identifiable cause; genetic + lifestyle
  • Secondary: 5–10%; identifiable cause (renal artery stenosis, primary aldosteronism, sleep apnea, thyroid disease)
RAAS: Renin → Angiotensin I → ACE → Angiotensin II → vasoconstriction + aldosterone → Na/water retention → ↑BP

Bowel Dysfunction

  • Reflex hypertonic (neurogenic): cervical/thoracic injury; empties reflexively without brain → incontinence
  • Flaccid hypotonic: lumbar injury; no reflex; sphincter relaxed → incontinence + constipation
Autonomic Dysreflexia: Emergency! Triggered by full bladder or bowel. Signs: sudden ↑BP, severe headache, bradycardia, profuse sweating above injury. Requires immediate intervention — find and remove the trigger.

Nursing Role

  • Document bowel/bladder patterns
  • Intermittent catheterization (IC) every 4–6 hours
  • Scheduled digital stimulation, suppositories
  • High fiber diet, adequate hydration
  • Emotional support and privacy
  • Refer to WOCN, dietitian, counselor

Ostomy Change Procedure

  1. Hand hygiene, gather supplies (gloves, bag, flange, scissors, measuring tool, skin prep, adhesive remover)
  2. Provide privacy
  3. Remove old system gently with adhesive remover; support surrounding skin
  4. Measure stool/urine output
  5. Clean stoma with warm water only (no soap — can irritate)
  6. Assess stoma color and surrounding skin
  7. Measure stoma diameter; cut flange opening to match (critical for ileostomy — enzymes destroy exposed skin)
  8. Apply skin prep/paste
  9. Press flange gently around stoma; attach bag
  10. Document: stoma color, moisture, skin integrity, patient teaching
High ostomy output (HOO): Output > intake within 3 weeks; more common with ileostomy; risk of dehydration/renal failure. Strict I&O; IV hydration. Change every 4–7 days; empty when ⅓ to ½ full.

5 Stages of Grief (Kübler-Ross)

  1. Denial — denying the feelings, not necessarily the facts
  2. Anger — normal, necessary; anger masks underlying pain
  3. Bargaining — "if this, then that"; can occur at any stage
  4. Depression — reality sets in; appropriate response, not mental illness
  5. Acceptance — not "okay" with loss, but adjusting to new reality

VALUE Framework

  • Value statements from family
  • Acknowledge emotions
  • Listen actively
  • Understand who patient is as a person
  • Elicit questions

Phases of Dying

Transition Phase (~2–3 weeks): Increased sleep · decreased appetite · difficulty swallowing (do NOT force feed) · withdrawal · may see deceased loved ones (normal) · incontinence begins
Actively Dying (~last 48 hours): Obtunded/comatose · BP drops · HR speeds up then slows · Cheyne-Stokes breathing · "Death rattle" (80% die within 48h of onset) · Mottling (starts lower extremities) · Minimal urine output
Nursing care: Hearing is LAST sense to go — keep talking positively. Mouth swabs for hydration near end. Schedule comfort meds proactively. Do NOT try to wake or force feed. Key documents: Advance directives · POLST · Durable Medical POA

Types of Pneumonia

TypeDefinition
CAPCommunity-acquired; outside hospital (includes assisted living)
HAPDevelops ≥48 hours after admission; not present on admission
VAPDevelops ≥48 hours after intubation
AspirationOropharyngeal or GI secretions enter lower respiratory tract

Common Organisms

  • Bacterial: S. pneumoniae (#1), H. influenzae, S. aureus, Mycoplasma ("walking pneumonia")
  • Viral: Influenza, SARS-CoV-2, RSV, rhinovirus
  • Fungal: P. jirovecii (HIV/immunocompromised), Aspergillus (VAP)

Signs & Symptoms

  • Fever/chills, body aches, fatigue, lack of appetite
  • Cough with or without sputum, shortness of breath
  • ↑HR, ↑RR, ↓O2 saturation, elevated WBC
  • Diminished/abnormal breath sounds
  • CXR: infiltrates/consolidation pattern
  • Older adults: Altered mental status may be the ONLY sign

Complications

  • Respiratory failure, lung abscess, empyema (pus in pleural space)
  • Atelectasis, bacteremia/sepsis → multi-organ failure
  • ARDS — life-threatening

Aspiration Prevention

  • Position upright (90°) during all meals
  • Chin-tuck maneuver — tuck chin to chest when swallowing to protect airway
  • Quiet environment with adequate lighting during meals
  • Thicken liquids per speech therapy recommendation
  • Serve food hot OR cold (stimulates swallow reflex better than room temp)
  • Regular oral care — reduces bacterial load (critical for ventilated patients)
  • Always elevate HOB for tube feedings; pause during repositioning
Dysphagia protocol: Nurse → bedside swallowing screen first → if suspected, NPO + notify provider + speech therapy referral. Speech therapy uses GUSS-ICU, barium swallow, FEES. May need to crush oral medications.
Empiric antibiotics: Piperacillin/tazobactam · cefepime · levofloxacin · or aminoglycoside + imipenem. Adjust based on cultures. Implement isolation per organism.
